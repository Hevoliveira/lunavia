# LUNAVIA for iPhone: this download

This folder installs **BUILD b6f976d**.

When it is installed, the first screen of LUNAVIA shows, at the top left:

> **LUNAVIA iOS · BUILD b6f976d · 2026-10-24 19:31 UTC**

It is the **manual lunar ascent**: after a safe landing (and the optional EVA) the ascent
preparation shows **LUNAR ASCENT — READY** (PT: ASCENSÃO LUNAR — PRONTA) with two choices, at all
four destinations and every difficulty:
- **GUIDED ASCENT** — the automatic liftoff exactly as before;
- **MANUAL ASCENT** — you fly the ascent stage off the descent stage (which stays on the Moon) into
  lunar orbit: ENGINE IGNITION (bottom left), PITCH ▲ / PITCH ▼ on the RCS (bottom right; the engine
  has fixed thrust and does not throttle), ENGINE CUTOFF when the apoapsis reaches about 96 km with
  a periapsis above 15 km. CADET has PITCH ASSIST and AUTO CUTOFF; ASTRONAUT the pitch cue, the time
  to cutoff and an optional AUTO CUTOFF; COMMANDER flies with no cue and no attitude hold.
  EXTERNAL, COCKPIT and NAV views; RETRY ASCENT after a failed attempt; the rendezvous then starts
  from the orbit you flew and the docking is yours as before.
Silent Horizon keeps EARTH LINK UNAVAILABLE (the onboard computer talks, Houston does not). The
orbit you reach is saved (ORBITAL INSERTION COMPLETE), the logbook records the manual ascent apart
from the 1,000-point score, and two new achievements (LUNAR ASCENT) mark it. The rest of the game is
as in BUILD 6b2b32a.

## Try the lunar EVA on the iPhone

Not yet tried on a physical iPhone; the checks below were run in a desktop browser.
1. Fly a mission to the **Sea of Tranquility** and land safely. On LUNAR SURFACE — ASCENT
   PREPARATION, wait for the checklist, then press **BEGIN EVA** (under INITIATE LUNAR ASCENT).
2. The hatch opens and the astronaut climbs down (SKIP › jumps to the surface). FIRST STEPS ON THE
   MOON appears.
3. **Left thumb:** put it down anywhere in the lower left and push to walk. **Right thumb:** drag to
   look around. The objectives are top left; the diamond marker shows where to go.
4. At the marked stone press **COLLECT SAMPLE**. At the photo point press **CAMERA**, turn until
   the whole LM is in the frame (the viewfinder says what is wrong), then the round shutter.
5. Walk back to the ladder and press **BOARD THE LM**. The astronaut climbs in, the hatch closes,
   EVA COMPLETE, and the ascent preparation is back: INITIATE LUNAR ASCENT as usual.
6. Close LUNAVIA in the middle of the EVA and open it again: RESUME MISSION brings you back to the
   EVA (at the foot of the ladder) with the objectives already done kept. After the EVA, it brings
   you back to the ascent preparation.
7. Walk far from the LM: at 24 m the objectives panel shows EXPLORATION LIMIT; at 31 m the
   astronaut stops. PAUSE (top right) stops everything.
8. Skipping the EVA (INITIATE LUNAR ASCENT straight away) flies the mission exactly as before.
9. **What to look at up close (BUILD 592b8c1):** drag the camera round the astronaut standing
   still: the gold visor reflects the ground and the LM (it updates after a couple of metres of
   walking), the chest unit's labels, the hoses, the backpack's panel; watch the boots while
   walking (heel first, contact shadows, treaded footprints); walk to the LM: the crinkled foil,
   the ladder's flat treads and the plaque on its strut, the ribbed footpads and the dark ring of
   disturbed dust round them, the hatch's handle. Note the frame rate while walking near the LM.

## Try save and resume on the iPhone

Not yet tried on a physical iPhone; the checks below were run in a desktop browser.
1. Start a mission (LAUNCH SEQUENCE — INITIATE, MISSIONS, a destination, a difficulty):
   MISSION CHECKPOINT SAVED · LUNAR DESCENT READY appears at the top.
2. Close LUNAVIA from the app switcher (swipe it up), open it again: THE ROAD TO THE MOON shows
   SAVED MISSION AVAILABLE (destination, difficulty, checkpoint, date and time). Nothing starts by
   itself. RESUME MISSION opens the descent briefing directly.
3. Repeat after the landing (resumes on the surface, ascent preparation), when the docking starts
   (resumes at the docking checkpoint), after HARD DOCK CONFIRMED (resumes on the way home) and at
   the start of the reentry (resumes the reentry). Closing in the middle of a maneuver resumes from
   the last safe checkpoint with RESUMED FROM LAST SAFE CHECKPOINT; a docking attempt you had
   started counts as a restart.
4. LAUNCH SEQUENCE — INITIATE with a saved mission asks START A NEW MISSION? (CANCEL keeps it).
5. After splashdown RESUME MISSION is gone; the debrief, records, medals and the mission log keep
   the flight.

The checkpoint is written to the app's own files (`MissionSaveStore.swift`, new in the Xcode
project) the moment it is reached; it does not depend on the app being closed cleanly.

- **This release makes the descent faster and finishes its look.** Nothing else changes: the reentry's physics, trajectory, controls, parachute timing and success rules, and everything before the reentry, are as in the previous release.
  - **Faster:** the sea computes each wave only where it can be seen (from altitude, none of the short ones), reads the distant cloud cover once per point of its grid instead of in every pixel, and stops the splash's ripples once they have died out; the clouds skip detail too small to see and draw their own buffer before the frame instead of in the middle of it (on a phone's GPU that saves writing the whole frame out and back).
  - **The near water:** wind ripples stretched across the wind, so the sea close to the capsule reads as wind-driven water, not mottled.
  - **The distant clouds:** squat fair-weather cumulus, as broad as they are tall, their bumps on the shoulders (no ball sitting on top).
  - **The released parachutes:** they fall away downwind, lie down on the water as they reach it (not before), bunched, and go under as one sheet, fading into deep water: no separate pieces, no pale patch.
- **What the descent looks like** (from the previous release, kept):
  - **Clouds:** real volumes, not flat pictures. Fair-weather cumulus with flat grey bases at one level (about 820 m), bright sunlit cauliflower tops, shaded sides and haze with distance. Under the drogues you look down on them; under the main parachutes the capsule falls between two of them, past their bases, into clear air over the sea.
  - **The descent, one continuous shot:** the drogues, then the main parachutes opening, the cloud layer, a wide shot of the whole parachute system over the sea, then down to the water close beside the capsule. The last part is shown slower (the same trajectory): about 24 s from the main parachutes to the water.
  - **Under the parachutes** the screen shows what matters then: altitude, descent rate, the parachutes (inflating, full), time to splashdown, the capsule and the heat shield, and the **SPLASHDOWN PREP** checklist (drogues, mains 3/3, VHF recovery beacon, RCS propellant dump, main release armed, brace). The lift-vector dial, roll and trim buttons and the entry corridor are gone there (the parachutes give no lift, so they did nothing). PAUSE and ABORT stay.
  - **Splashdown:** a burst of spray thrown out round the heat shield, then, in the water itself, the surface pushed down and rebounding, ripples that run out and fade, churned white water, and a ragged lace of foam round the floating capsule. No rings, no discs.
  - **Then:** the camera comes round and in, low over the water, to the floating capsule, rocking on the swell; SPLASHDOWN CONFIRMED, CREW SAFE and LUNAVIA — MISSION COMPLETE over the same shot.
  - **Captions** ("We have splashdown... Welcome home") keep their words, languages and timing but sit small in the upper-left corner under the phase banner, clear of the capsule.

- **How a mission goes after the landing** (as in the previous release):
  1. After a successful landing, **CONTINUE MISSION** shows **LUNAR SURFACE — ASCENT PREPARATION**: the checklist ticks in, and nothing starts until you press **INITIATE LUNAR ASCENT**.
  2. A short countdown, then **PROCEED**. The ascent stage leaves the descent stage, which stays on the Moon with its legs, and climbs away. SKIP CINEMATIC goes on to orbit.
  3. **Orbit insertion** and the **automatic rendezvous**: the radar range falls from 48 km as the guidance flies the terminal phase, a midcourse correction and the braking. SKIP CINEMATIC goes to the hand-over.
  4. **LUNAVIA — DOCKING OPERATIONS:** you fly the ascent stage onto the CSM's docking probe.
     - Left pad: **▲ ▼ ◀ ▶** move it up, down, left and right. Right buttons: **FWD / AFT** (closing speed).
     - **MODE** switches the pad to **ATTITUDE** (pitch and yaw; the right buttons roll).
     - A tap is one small RCS pulse; holding keeps the jets firing. Two fingers work together.
     - **NAV | WINDOW | COCKPIT | 360°** choose the view (in NAV, the cyan circle on the reticle is the probe tip).
     - The panel top right shows your port's offset (dot) and the attitude (cross) against the capture circle, INSIDE / OUTSIDE CORRIDOR, and the capture conditions (all green: PROCEED WITH FINAL APPROACH).
     - CADET: **AUTO-ALIGN**, **STABILIZE** and the **APPROACH SPEED ASSIST** help (see above). ASTRONAUT: the attitude holds still when you let go, and **STABILIZE** cancels a drift once. COMMANDER: nothing holds; you stop what you start.
     - Too fast, off the axis, misaligned or rotating: no capture, and the vehicles push apart. Back off and try again. A hard impact or a collision ends the attempt: **RESTART DOCKING** (no new landing or launch).
  5. **HARD DOCK CONFIRMED**, crew transfer, the ascent stage jettisoned, then the return and reentry as before. MISSION COMPLETE needs the docking.
  - Silent Horizon (far side): no Houston on the surface or after insertion, until the rendezvous brings you back into view of the Earth. The guidance panel works throughout.
